[0050] ABSTRACT
[0051] Financial mismanagement remained a critical and documented challenge among Filipino households, compounded by limited access to accessible, localized, and intelligent financial tools. This study aimed to: (1) assess the financial management practices, budgeting challenges, and expense-tracking behaviors of parents aged 35–55 and young professionals aged 21–35 in San Fernando City, La Union; (2) design and develop SmartSpend, an AI-assisted mobile financial tracking and advisory application for Android; and (3) evaluate the usability of the developed system using the System Usability Scale (SUS).
[0052] The study employed a mixed-methods developmental research design. A validated survey questionnaire was administered to assess financial practices and technology acceptance. SmartSpend was developed using an Agile Kanban methodology, integrating a multi-provider large language model (LLM) architecture with Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite as the primary model, enabling 34 autonomous financial management actions. Usability was evaluated using the 10-item Brooke (1996) SUS instrument administered after respondents used the application for at least 10 minutes.
[0053] The needs assessment revealed that 96.7% of respondents expressed positive intent to use an AI-powered financial application, and 93.3% already used GCash, validating the target features. SmartSpend version 3.1.0 was successfully developed as an offline-first Android application with multi-modal expense input, a Financial Health Score (FHS) formally classified as a Prototype Observed Financial Health Indicator, and a six-provider AI failover chain. Usability evaluation yielded a mean SUS score of 61.83 (SD = 17.58), corresponding to a grade of D and an adjective rating of "OK" under the Bangor et al. (2009) scale below the 68-point benchmark (Sauro & Lewis, 2016) and the pre-established target of 80.
[0054] The results indicated that SmartSpend showed promise as a prototype AI-assisted financial tool for the target demographic, though the below-target usability score highlighted areas for UI/UX refinement. The study contributed a locally contextualized, evaluated prototype that addressed gaps in existing Philippine personal finance applications, particularly the absence of Filipino-English LLM integration, offline-first operation, and behavioral health scoring. Seven recommendations were identified for future development, including UI/UX refinement, a backend API proxy, on-device AI, SQLite encryption, formal parsing evaluation, and a longitudinal study.

[0261] Ethical Considerations 
[0262] All ethical considerations were strictly followed throughout the entire research process. Participation in this study was voluntary, and no form of pressure was imposed on the respondents. All participants, including parents and young professionals, had the right to refuse participation or withdraw from the study at any time without any penalty.
[0263] Informed consent was obtained from all participants before their involvement in the study. They were properly informed about the nature and purpose of the research prior to their participation.
[0264] In addition, confidentiality was strictly maintained. Personal information of the respondents was kept secure and treated with utmost confidentiality. All responses gathered were used solely for academic purposes.
[0265] Furthermore, all collected data were carefully managed and stored to prevent any form of loss or unauthorized access. The researchers ensured that ethical standards were upheld throughout the study.
