    1 — What is this app and why does it matter?
    What problem does SmartSpend solve?

    Most Filipinos don't track their finances because it's tedious — manually opening an app, filling forms, picking categories, remembering amounts. SmartSpend removes that friction: you just talk to it naturally (I spent 60 for lunch and 30 for jeep) and it logs both expenses automatically. For Filipino students and young professionals already on their phones all day, this lowers the barrier to building a financial habit.

    Who is your target user?

    Two primary groups: (1) Filipino college students with irregular allowances — handled by the allowance cycle and student account type. (2) Young working professionals aged 21–35 who earn a salary but have never tracked finances. Designed mobile-first, AI-assisted, to fit existing phone habits.

    How is this different from BudgetPH, PISO, or Agila?

    vs BudgetPH/PISO: They have no AI — you log manually. We support voice, OCR, barcode, and screenshot import. We're also the only one with batch screenshot import from 40+ platforms.

    vs Agila: Agila has an "Agila Chat" feature but its AI is logging-only — it can't set budgets, track debts, compute savings, or give advice. Our 34 agentic actions cover all of that.

    Unique to SmartSpend: The dual-mode Financial Health Score, paluwagan tracker, SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG tracking, and PH-specific content (150+ Filipino item catalog, 20 PH banks).

    What makes this a research contribution, not just another app?

    Three novel contributions: (1) Dual-mode FHS — adapts its formula based on whether the user tracks income. No other Filipino finance app has a validated scoring system. (2) Multi-modal AI with 34 agentic actions in Taglish — combining voice, OCR, barcode, and screenshot import in one free app. (3) 8-provider AI fallback chain — a resilience architecture for maintaining AI availability across multiple free-tier providers, practical for apps that can't afford paid subscriptions.

    2 — The Financial Health Score (FHS)
    Explain the FHS formula simply — for the panel

    The score has 4 equal components, 25 points each, totaling 100:

    Savings Rate (25 pts)
    = 25 × min(1, savings_rate / 0.20)
    Full points when saving ≥20% of income. Scales down proportionally below that.

    Overspend Control (25 pts)
    = 25 × (days_within_budget / days_elapsed)
    How many days did spending stay under the daily budget?

    Budget Adherence (25 pts)
    = 25 × (categories_on_track / total_budgeted)
    No budgets set → full 25 pts. We don't penalize new users.

    Logging Consistency (25 pts)
    = 25 × (days_logged / days_elapsed_this_month)
    How regularly are you logging expenses?

    Total is clamped 0–100. ≥80 = Excellent, 60–79 = Good, <60 = Needs attention.

    What about students with no fixed income?

    Two modes: Full Mode uses all 4 components including Savings Rate, which needs income data. Lightweight Mode (one toggle in Settings) replaces income-dependent components with spending-habit ones: Spending Restraint, Logging Consistency, Category Balance, and Habit Streak. A meaningful 0–100 score with no salary input required.

    Why 20% as the savings target?

    From the 50/30/20 framework — 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings — widely recognized in personal finance and cited in BSP financial literacy materials. Gives users a clear, researched goal rather than an arbitrary number.

    How does the score handle logging gaps — forgotten days?

    Logging Gap Detection: when days show no log but wallet balance changed (suggesting spending happened), the app asks "Did you have transactions on those days?" Confirming yes applies a small Logging Consistency penalty. Confirming a genuine no-spend day gives a small bonus. More accurate than silently ignoring missing days.

    How is the FHS different from a credit score?

    A credit score measures creditworthiness — whether you pay loans on time. The FHS measures financial behavior — how well you manage your day-to-day spending. It's a self-improvement tool, not a financial product. It resets monthly (new month, fresh start) and explains exactly which component is dragging the score down so users know what to fix.

    3 — How the AI works
    Is the AI safe? Does it send our financial data to external servers?

    The AI receives only the text you type and a summarized expense context — categories and amounts, no names, account numbers, or personal identifiers. Mobile numbers are automatically redacted before sending (RA 10173 compliance). The actual expense data lives locally in SQLite and never leaves the phone except for encrypted Firebase sync.

    What happens when the AI is down or the internet is slow?

    An 8-provider fallback chain automatically switches: Gemini → 5 Groq models → Cerebras. Each has its own daily limit. The app tracks and persists the active provider across restarts. If all 8 fail, all manual entry features still work 100% offline — log, view, analyze, budgets, wallets.

    How do you prevent AI categorization mistakes?

    Three layers: (1) Evidence threshold — if "Sting" has been logged as Food 10 times, one uncertain AI response can't reclassify it as Others. Requires ≥3 prior entries and ≥60% agreement. (2) Action allowlist — import/OCR flows can only trigger expense-logging actions, not delete records or modify account settings. (3) Confidence score — every AI-logged expense gets a confidence score 0–1; below 70% shows a "Review" badge so users know to double-check.

    Who pays for the AI? It must cost money.

    We use free tiers across providers — Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite via Google AI Studio, Groq and Cerebras free APIs. We enforce 150 messages/day per user across all providers — zero cost for a research prototype. API keys are stored in Firebase Remote Config (not in the APK), so rotation requires no app rebuild. Production scaling would move to paid tiers via the same Remote Config infrastructure.

    How is this different from just using ChatGPT?

    ChatGPT has no persistent data — context is lost each session. SmartSpend maintains a complete on-device financial database (218 expenses, 5 wallets, 3 savings goals in our test account) and executes 34 action types: saves to DB, updates wallet balances, sends notifications, calculates FHS scores. ChatGPT can chat; it can't do any of that. Also — ChatGPT Finance is US-only, requires Plaid bank linking, and isn't available in the Philippines.

    4 — Technical implementation
    Why Flutter and not native Android or React Native?

    Flutter gives a single codebase for Android, iOS, and web. For our Android-only capstone scope, it also gave first-party access to Google ML Kit for OCR and barcode scanning. Dart iterates faster than Kotlin for UI work — mattered given our 1-semester timeline. The app is already structured to support iOS and web with minimal changes post-capstone.

    How does batch screenshot import work technically?

    User selects multiple gallery screenshots. Each runs through Google ML Kit text recognition on-device — no internet needed for OCR. The extracted text goes to the AI with a structured prompt to identify item, amount, date, merchant. The AI returns JSON; the app displays it for user confirmation before saving. 40+ platforms are supported because we've built keyword detection for their specific receipt formats.

    How is data stored and is it secure?

    Primary storage: SQLite on-device (schema v11, 20 tables). Cloud backup: Firebase Firestore (user-controlled). API keys: NOT in the APK — fetched from Firebase Remote Config at startup. Firebase App Check blocks unauthorized API access. Local security: PIN or biometric lock via local_auth.

    How many screens and services does the app have?

    42 screens (Dart files in lib/screens), 31 services (lib/services), 20 SQLite tables, 3 model classes. Key services: DBService (all database ops), AIChatService (LLM + action parsing), AppConfig (model routing + Remote Config), ScoreService (FHS formula), StartupAlertsService (on-open alerts), ProactiveNudgeService (forward-looking push notifications).

    5 — Research and methodology
    What is your research methodology?

    Agile Kanban development — tasks move through Backlog → In Progress → Done in sprints. Evaluation: System Usability Scale (SUS), a standardized 10-item questionnaire (0–100), 30 respondents post-defense (20 parents, 10 young professionals). Target: above 80 — maps to "Good" on the Bangor et al. (2009) adjective scale. The 30 respondents also serve as the Google Play closed testing pool (requires ≥12 testers for ≥14 days before production eligibility).

    What are your research questions?

    In general: Does AI-assisted expense tracking improve financial awareness among Filipino students and young professionals? Specifically: (1) Does multi-modal input reduce the friction of expense logging? (2) Does the Financial Health Score provide actionable feedback? (3) Does the app achieve a SUS score ≥80?

    How does the app comply with Philippine laws?

    RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act): PII redaction before AI, data stored locally by default, privacy policy disclosing collection/use/rights. RA 11765 (Financial Consumer Protection): One-time AI advice disclaimer clarifying the app is not a licensed financial adviser. BSP OFxPERA: The app's architecture is compatible with Open Finance — wallet and transaction data is structured to support API integration if it becomes mandatory.

    6 — Hard questions / limitations
    What are the limitations of your app?

    (1) AI works best with clear input — ambiguous messages can produce wrong categories; mitigated by the evidence threshold but not perfect. (2) No Play Store release yet — Google's 14-day closed testing is scheduled post-defense. (3) No iOS version — Flutter supports it but requires a Mac with Xcode. (4) FHS formula not yet externally validated with real user data — SUS survey and post-defense testing will address this. (5) Wallet balance tracking requires manual updates for unlogged cash transactions.

    What happens after the 150 message/day AI limit?

    A clear message shows that the daily limit is reached, resetting midnight UTC. All manual features stay fully functional — Log Expense, Edit, Budgets, Analytics, Hub tools all work without AI. The 150-message limit across 8 providers means ~1,200 total capacity per day across all users — sufficient for a research prototype.

    Why isn't it on the Play Store yet?

    Google Play now requires new personal developer accounts to run a closed testing track with ≥12 active testers for ≥14 consecutive days before production access. This is a mandatory new policy from 2024. We've structured the SUS survey respondents to double as the closed testing pool — both happen post-defense simultaneously.

    What's the future roadmap?

    Post-capstone v3.x priorities: (1) Experience presets (Lite/Casual/Normal/Pro mode system). (2) Play Store submission. (3) iOS version. (4) Investment tracker (MP2, PSE, UITFs). (5) Tablet/landscape layout. (6) Price intelligence using PSA commodity data. We also plan to expand the wallet system with per-wallet transaction history and wallet reconciliation prompts.
