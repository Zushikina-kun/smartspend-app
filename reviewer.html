    ⚠️ Version Discrepancy — How to Answer This

    The panel may notice the manuscript, printed slides, and the actual app are on different versions. Here is exactly how to answer every variation of this question.

That is the research version — it cannot change after evaluation. The app on the phone is v3.3.1, which is the current development version that continues after the research phase. Think of it like a published study evaluating a product at a specific point in time — the product keeps evolving but the study findings remain valid for the version studied.

    ❓ "Your printed slides show features that don't match what's on the app now."

    The slides were printed on November 16, the original defense date. Between November 16 and today (November 20), we continued development based on our panel's recommendations — specifically implementing Recommendation 1 (payday-cycle budgeting) and three UI improvements. The slides reflect the state at the time of printing; the app reflects our active response to the research findings.

    This is actually a strength: it shows the team actively improves the system based on research feedback rather than treating the app as finished.

    The manuscript documents the system as evaluated in the study (v3.1.0, October 2026). Features added after evaluation — Government Contributions Tracker (v3.2.0), OpenRouter AI gateway (v3.2.5), payday-cycle budgeting (v3.3.0) — are post-research enhancements that respond to our panel recommendations and roadmap.     The manuscript was written and printed when the app had 9 providers. We integrated OpenRouter as a 10th provider on November 16, 2026 — after printing. The honest approach — which we took — is to document the current version's improvements in the CHANGELOG and propose a longitudinal evaluation as Recommendation 7.

    ⭐ 1 — The 11 Questions They Will Definitely Ask
    Why is the SUS score only 61.83? SUS critical

    Our SUS score of 61.83 is below the 68 benchmark, which is why Objective 3 is partially achieved rather than fully achieved. The score was pulled down by consistency and complexity questions — not by people disliking the app, since they scored high on wanting to use it again (4.35/5). It is an honest prototype-stage finding that tells us exactly what to fix, which is what good developmental research is supposed to do.

    Why only 30 respondents? Isn't that too small? SUS research

    SUS is built to be reliable with small samples — research shows it stabilizes around 12–14 participants, and our 30 is well above that. For a one-semester capstone, 30 is a common and defensible sample size. We are upfront that it limits how far we can generalize, which is exactly why we recommend a larger, longer follow-up study.

    What LLM did you use and why? AI critical

    We evaluated Gemini Flash-Lite as the main model because it handled Taglish well, was fast, and had a generous free tier. We did not commit to one model, though — the app now auto-falls back across 10 providers so the AI keeps working even when one is down. Full technical details are in the Technical Reference section below.

    How does SmartSpend compare to BudgetPH, PISO, Agila? research

    BudgetPH and PISO have no AI — you log everything by hand. Agila has an AI chat, but it can only log expenses; it cannot set budgets, track debts, or give advice. SmartSpend is the only one whose AI actually performs 34 different actions, plus Filipino-specific features like SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG tracking and paluwagan.

    Is this quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods? research critical

    It is purely quantitative descriptive developmental — no interviews, no open-ended data, no mixed methods. We used validated structured surveys for both the needs assessment and the SUS usability evaluation, and we developed the app using an Agile Kanban workflow.

    How secure is the app? Where are API keys stored? security critical

    Your expense data lives only on your phone by default, and the API keys are not baked into the app — they are fetched securely from Firebase at startup. Before anything goes to the AI, phone numbers and account patterns are redacted automatically. We honestly acknowledge two gaps still open — the local database is not encrypted yet, and there is no server-side proxy — both are in our recommendations.

    What about iOS? Why Android only? tech

    Android owns 70–80% of the Philippine market, especially among students and budget-conscious users, so that is where the impact is. iOS also needs a Mac and a paid developer account, which did not fit a one-semester capstone budget. Flutter makes an iOS version easy to add later, but it is outside our current scope.

    How does the Financial Health Score work? Is it validated? FHS critical

    It is a 0–100 score from four parts — savings rate, overspend control, budget adherence, and logging consistency — worth 25 points each. We are transparent that it is a prototype indicator, not a validated instrument; the equal weights are a design choice. A longer follow-up study is what would properly validate it.

    What are all 9 recommendations? research critical

    The nine recommendations are: (1) payday-cycle budgeting, (2) a backend proxy for API keys, (3) Google Play release, (4) on-device AI, (5) database encryption, (6) household and remittance features, (7) a longer follow-up evaluation, (8) UI/UX refinement, and (9) a formal receipt-parsing evaluation. We have already implemented #1 between defenses.

    How do you handle offline mode? tech

    All the core features work with no internet — logging, viewing, editing, budgets, wallets, analytics, and receipt scanning all run on-device. Only the AI chat needs a connection. We call it offline-first on purpose, because the app’s main job never fails when you lose signal.

    🧮 7 — Financial Health Score (FHS) Explained
    Explain the FHS formula simply — for the panel FHS critical

    The score has 4 equal components, 25 points each, totaling 100. We formally classify this as a Prototype Observed Financial Health Indicator, not a validated instrument. Equal weights are a design choice, not statistically derived.

    Savings Rate (25 pts)
    = 25 × min(1, savings_rate / 0.20)
    Full points when saving ≥20% of income. Scales proportionally below.

    Overspend Control (25 pts)
    = 25 × (days_within_budget / days_elapsed)
    How many days did spending stay under daily budget?

    Budget Adherence (25 pts)
    = 25 × (categories_on_track / total_budgeted)
    No budgets set → full 25 pts. We don’t penalize new users.

    Logging Consistency (25 pts)
    = 25 × (days_logged / days_elapsed_this_month)
    How regularly are you logging expenses?

    Total clamped 0–100. ≥80 = Good, 60–79 = Fair, <60 = Needs attention.

    Two adjustments: Warning Decay (-up to 15 pts for discretionary overspending), Gap Adjustment (+2 confirmed no-spend day, -3 unlogged day).

    What about students with no fixed income? FHS

    Two modes: Full Mode uses all 4 components including Savings Rate (needs income data). Lightweight Mode (one toggle in Settings) replaces income-dependent components with spending-habit ones: Spending Restraint, Logging Consistency, Category Balance, and Habit Streak. Provides a meaningful 0–100 score with no salary input required.

    Why 20% as the savings target? FHS

    From the 50/30/20 framework — 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings — widely recognized in personal finance and cited in BSP financial literacy materials. Provides a clear, researched goal rather than an arbitrary number.

    How is the FHS different from a credit score? FHS

    A credit score measures creditworthiness — whether you pay loans on time. The FHS measures financial behavior — how well you manage day-to-day spending. It is a self-improvement tool, not a financial product. It resets monthly (new month, fresh start) and explains which component is dragging the score down so users know what to fix.

    Is the FHS a validated instrument? FHS critical

    No — and we are transparent about this. We formally classify the FHS as a Prototype Observed Financial Health Indicator. The equal 25-point weights are a design choice, not statistically derived. We adapted ideas from the CBA-MI Observed scale and the Financial Health Network’s FinHealth Score. Recommendation 7 (longitudinal evaluation) would eventually provide data to validate or refine the formula with real-world data.

    What is paluwagan and why include a tracker? AI

    Paluwagan is a traditional Filipino rotating savings group — a fixed number of members each contribute a set amount every period (weekly, monthly), and one member receives the full pot per period until everyone has had a turn. SmartSpend’s paluwagan tracker: (1) Tracks contribution schedule (who pays when), (2) Records received payouts vs. outstanding contributions, (3) Calculates net position (how much you’ve paid vs. received), (4) Sends reminders before contribution due dates. This is culturally specific — no international app has paluwagan support.

    How did you calculate the SUS score? Walk through the formula. SUS

    SUS uses a 10-item questionnaire (Brooke, 1996). Each item is rated 1–5 (Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree). Scoring: (1) For odd-numbered items (1,3,5,7,9): response - 1. (2) For even-numbered items (2,4,6,8,10): 5 - response. (3) Sum all 10 contributions and multiply by 2.5. Formula: SUS = 2.5 × (Σodd_contributions + Σeven_contributions). Result range: 0–100. Our mean across 30 valid respondents: 61.83.

    Figure 16 in the manuscript shows a sample computation for one illustrative respondent that yielded 75.0 — this is only the example. The actual study mean is 61.83.

    How do you justify 30 respondents statistically? SUS research

    Three justifications: (1) SUS validation literature — Tullis & Stetson (2004) found SUS produces reliable results with as few as 12–14 participants; our 30 is well above this. (2) Capstone scope — developmental research at this level commonly uses 20–30 respondents for prototype evaluation. (3) Practical constraint — recruiting participants who would use the app for 10+ minutes (minimum) and complete a formal survey is resource-intensive for a 1-semester project. We acknowledge this limits generalizability, hence Recommendation 7 (longitudinal study with larger sample).

    Why use SUS and not a custom usability questionnaire? SUS research

    SUS is the industry-standard usability metric: (1) Validated — 30+ years of research, 5,000+ studies. (2) Standardized — 10 questions, Likert 1–5, consistent algorithm. (3) Benchmarked — Average SUS = 68; ≥80 = "Good" (Bangor 2009). (4) Quick — 2–3 minutes per respondent, minimizes survey fatigue. (5) Panel-recognizable — Panelists will know and trust SUS. A custom questionnaire would have no normative data for comparison and would require its own validation process.

    How did you exclude the 6 invalid SUS respondents? SUS research

    Exclusion criteria were defined before data collection: (1) 5 excluded for insufficient usage time — respondents who completed the SUS survey but had not used the app for the required minimum of 10 minutes. Usage time was verified via self-report on the survey form and cross-checked against app session logs where available. (2) 1 excluded for straight-line responding — a respondent who selected the same response (e.g., all 3s) for all 10 SUS items, indicating non-engagement with the instrument. This reduces the valid sample from 36 to 30.

    What are the specific SUS item results? SUS

    Strongest items (highest scores):

    • Q1 "I think I would like to use this system frequently" — mean 4.35/5 (intent to use)

    Weakest items (lowest scores, driving the score below benchmark):

    • Q6 "I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system" — weakness (consistency concerns)
    • Q2 "I found the system unnecessarily complex" — weakness (complexity)
    • Q4 "I think I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system" — weakness (complexity)

    These item-level results directly inform Recommendation 8 (UI/UX refinement targeting consistency and complexity).

    What is paluwagan and how does SmartSpend handle it? tech

    Paluwagan (also called "hulugan" or "kamustahan" in some regions) is a traditional Filipino rotating savings club. A fixed group of members each contribute a fixed amount every period (weekly or monthly). Each period, one member receives the entire pooled amount ("pot"), rotating until every member has received it once. It functions as a zero-interest, trust-based savings and lending mechanism widely used by Filipinos who lack access to formal bank loans or investment accounts. SmartSpend's paluwagan tracker stores: group name, number of members, contribution amount, cycle period, and each member's scheduled payout position. It auto-generates the full contribution schedule, tracks which members have paid each round, records when you receive your payout, calculates your net position (total paid vs. total received), and sends push reminders before each contribution due date. No international personal finance app offers paluwagan support — this is a culturally specific feature unique to SmartSpend.

    How does Safe-to-Spend calculate its number exactly? FHS tech

    The formula is: Safe-to-Spend = current_balance − upcoming_bills − savings_shortfall − overdue_debts − buffer. Each component is defined precisely: current_balance is the sum of all wallet balances in the wallets table; upcoming_bills is the sum of bill amounts due within the next 7 days from the bills table; savings_shortfall is how much more needs to be saved this month to stay on track for all active savings goals (target_amount / months_remaining, minus amount_saved_this_month); overdue_debts is the minimum payment due on any debt past its due_date; and buffer is a user-configurable emergency reserve defaulting to 10% of monthly income. The result is shown on the Home screen in real time. A negative result shows in red with a "Warning: overspent" message; positive shows in green. The concept was inspired by PocketGuard's "In My Pocket" feature but extended to include debt and savings-goal considerations specific to Filipino financial patterns.

    Full Mode vs Lightweight Mode FHS — what is the difference? FHS

    Full Mode is the default and requires income tracking. Its four components are: Savings Rate (how close to 20% of income are you saving?), Overspend Control (how many days did spending stay within daily budget?), Budget Adherence (how many category budgets are on track?), and Logging Consistency (how many days this month did you log at least one expense?). All four are weighted 25 points each for a 100-point total. Lightweight Mode (toggled in Settings) replaces income-dependent components for users with no fixed income such as students or gig workers. Its four components are: Spending Restraint (did daily spending stay below a self-set daily cap?), Logging Consistency (same as Full Mode), Category Balance (is spending spread across essential categories rather than concentrated in one?), and Habit Streak (consecutive days with at least one logged expense). The toggle allows the same 0–100 scale to be meaningful regardless of whether the user earns a salary, an allowance, or irregular freelance income. This dual-mode design is one of the three novel contributions stated in the manuscript.

    How does the app handle multiple currencies? tech

    SmartSpend supports 57 currencies for display and manual entry. The primary operating currency is Philippine Peso (PHP), and all wallet balances, budgets, and analytics default to PHP. For multi-currency use, the app stores a currency code alongside each wallet — users can create a USD wallet for dollar savings, a EUR wallet for travel funds, etc. Exchange rates are fetched from a free exchange rate API when internet is available and cached locally for offline display. The AI understands currency mentions in Taglish messages ("nagbayad ako ng 20 dollars sa Steam") and converts to PHP at the cached rate before logging. Analytics and the FHS always compute in the user's home currency (PHP by default) using the cached conversion. Full multi-currency consolidation (live rates, automatic conversion on every transaction, consolidated net worth in one currency) is part of the post-capstone OFW remittance feature in the roadmap.

    What is the app download size (APK)? tech

    The release APK for the ARM64 architecture (arm64-v8a) is approximately ~46 MB. This is the architecture used by most modern Android phones (2018 onward). The build uses --split-per-abi, so ARM32 (armeabi-v7a) and x86_64 builds are separate files, each smaller than a universal APK would be. Size breakdown: Flutter engine (~12 MB), app Dart code and assets (~8 MB), Google ML Kit OCR model (~18 MB), Firebase SDKs (~5 MB), and other dependencies (~3 MB). Post-capstone, distributing via Google Play as an AAB (Android App Bundle) would allow Play Asset Delivery to serve only the ML Kit model slices needed for the specific device, potentially reducing the installed size to under 30 MB on most phones.

    How does the evidence-based categorization system prevent AI mistakes? tech AI

    Three layers: (1) Evidence threshold — if "Sting" has been logged as Food 10 times, one uncertain AI response cannot reclassify it as Others. Requires ≥3 prior entries and ≥60% category agreement. Stored in evidence_log table and evidence_category_map. (2) Action allowlist — OCR/import flows can only trigger expense-logging actions, not delete records or modify account settings. The AI action parser validates action type against an allowlist before execution. (3) Confidence score — every AI-logged expense gets a confidence score 0–1; below 70% shows a "Review" badge so users know to double-check. This three-layer system gives users trust in AI-categorized expenses while catching edge cases.

    How does the Safe-to-Spend calculation work? tech FHS

    Formula: Safe-to-Spend = current_balance - upcoming_bills - savings_goal_shortfall - overdue_debts - buffer

    Components:

    • Current balance: sum of all wallet balances
    • Upcoming bills: sum of bill amounts due in the next 7 days (from bills table)
    • Savings goal shortfall: how much more needs to be saved this month to stay on track for all active goals
    • Overdue debts: minimum payment due on any debt that is past due date
    • Buffer: user-configurable emergency reserve (default 10% of income)

    The result is displayed prominently on the Home screen. If negative, the card shows red with "Warning: overspent." If positive, green with the safe amount. This is the same concept as PocketGuard’s core feature — we acknowledge the inspiration and differentiate by adding debt and savings goal considerations.

    How does the proactive notification system work? tech AI

    SmartSpend uses three notification types: (1) Daily nudge (2 PM) — if no expense logged today, Peso sends "Hey! No expenses logged yet today — did you spend anything?" Implemented via ProactiveNudgeService using local notifications (flutter_local_notifications). (2) Bill reminders — 3 days before a bill is due, automatic push notification. (3) Budget alerts — when a category reaches 80% and 100% of budget, Peso warns. Smart logic: Notifications check user_settings.notifications_enabled and respect Do Not Disturb hours (9 PM–8 AM). They are also contextual — if the user already logged an expense via AI chat, the 2 PM nudge is cancelled for that day.

    Explain the AI privacy tier system in technical detail. security tech

    Tier 1 — Cloud AI (default): PII redaction regex runs on-device before the HTTP request is built. Redacted patterns: Philippine mobile numbers (09XX format), reference numbers (12+ digit strings), account number patterns. The redacted message is sent to the selected cloud provider. One-time privacy_disclosure_shown flag in user_settings controls the first-run consent dialog.

    Tier 2 — Private Local Mode: AppConfig.localAIUrl points to the user’s PC (default: http://192.168.x.x:11434/api/chat for Ollama). Requests go directly to the local server — never through cloud APIs. If the local server is unreachable (timeout <3s), the app falls back to cloud unless the user enabled "Local-only mode" toggle, in which case AI simply returns "Local AI unavailable — please check your server."

    Tier 3 — On-device AI: NOT implemented. Would require a ~2.6GB model download (Recommendation 4). Would run entirely on-phone via Google’s LiteRT-LM API, with zero network calls for AI.
