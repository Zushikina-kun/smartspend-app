## 💬 Quick Defense Answers

**Q: Why Grade D / below 68?**
→ "61.83 falls in the marginal range, below both the 68 industry average (Sauro & Lewis 2016) and our 80 target. We acknowledge this as a prototype limitation. The SUS results identified consistency and complexity as the main areas for UI/UX improvement, which is Recommendation 5."

**Q: Why only 30 respondents?**
→ "We used purposive sampling targeting a specific demographic in San Fernando City. We followed valid exclusion criteria — 5 for insufficient app-use time and 1 for straight-line response bias — leaving 30 valid responses from 36."

**Q: Why 10 parents but 20 young professionals in SUS?**
→ "The SUS and needs-assessment samples were separate. In the SUS sample, 10 respondents self-identified as parents and 20 as young professionals. Parents scored 58.25 and young professionals 63.62. A larger parent sample is part of our longitudinal recommendation."

**Q: Why is Objective 3 partially achieved?**
→ "We successfully evaluated usability using the SUS with 30 valid respondents — the evaluation itself was completed. However, the score of 61.83 fell below both the 68 average and our 80 target. So the objective was carried out, but the target was not met."

**Q: Is the data private?**
→ "Mobile numbers, reference numbers, and account patterns are redacted on-device before any cloud AI call. The local database is not yet encrypted — that is Recommendation 4. SmartSpend is designed with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 in mind."

**Q: Is the FHS valid?**
→ "It is a Prototype Observed Financial Health Indicator. It follows the transaction-data approach of the CBA–Melbourne Institute and borrows Spend/Save/Plan ideas from the FinHealth Score. The weights are a design choice, not statistically derived — that is why it is a prototype and why longitudinal validation is Recommendation 7."
