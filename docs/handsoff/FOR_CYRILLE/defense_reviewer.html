Financial Health Score (FHS) — 4 Components

1. Savings Rate (25 pts)
Based on 20% savings target (50/30/20 rule)
2. Overspend Control (25 pts)
Tracks habitual vs. one-off overspend days
3. Budget Adherence (25 pts)
On-budget categories ÷ total budgeted categories
4. Logging Consistency (25 pts)
Logged days ÷ active days this period
FHS is formally classified as a Prototype Observed Financial Health Indicator — adapted from CBA-MI and FinHealth Network frameworks. It is NOT presented as a validated instrument.

💡 9 Recommendations

1
Payday-cycle-aware budgeting

Implement budget resets aligned with the Philippine semi-monthly (15th and 30th) salary cycle.

2
Backend API proxy and per-user keys

Move LLM API key to a server-side proxy (e.g., Firebase Cloud Function) to remove device-side exposure. Free-tier quotas are currently shared by all users of the prototype.

3
Google Play release

After backend proxy and privacy policy are in place, submit to the Google Play Store. Currently distributed as a downloadable APK.

4
On-device AI tier

Add an opt-in on-device model (e.g., Gemma 4 E2B via Google's LiteRT-LM, ~2.6 GB download) for fully offline AI without network dependency.

5
SQLite encryption

Implement SQLCipher-based encryption in a future schema migration. Current local database is not encrypted.

6
Household and remittance features

Add shared couple/family wallets and OFW remittance tracking as a distinct income category.

7
Longitudinal evaluation

Measure SmartSpend's effect on financial behavior over 3–6 months with a larger sample. Current SUS measured usability at a single point in time.

8
UI/UX refinement

Address SUS concerns for consistency (Q6) and complexity (Q2) through targeted interface redesign to improve the overall usability score.

9
Formal parsing evaluation

Conduct formal evaluation of OCR and AI parsing accuracy using 50+ standardized receipts across multiple platforms to establish a performance baseline.

❓ Anticipated Panel Questions & Answers

Why did SmartSpend score only 61.83 — is that a failure? ▼

The score of 61.83 falls within the Marginal range under Bangor et al. (2008) and corresponds to an adjective rating of "OK" under Bangor et al. (2009). While this is below our pre-established target of 80 and below the 68-point average benchmark (Sauro & Lewis, 2016), it does not constitute a failure of the system.

SmartSpend is a prototype, and prototype-stage systems commonly yield Marginal SUS scores. The evaluation successfully identified specific areas for improvement — particularly system consistency (Q6) and complexity (Q2 and Q4) — which directly informed our UI/UX recommendations. The third objective was partially achieved: usability was evaluated, the score fell below target, and the gap is documented and addressed in our recommendations.

Why only 30 respondents? Is that enough? ▼

Thirty respondents is aligned with established practice for capstone-level usability and needs-assessment studies (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2011). For SUS specifically, Nielsen (1993) demonstrated that 5 respondents are sufficient to identify usability issues, and studies published in Atlantis Press (2026) similarly support 20–30 as sufficient for formative usability evaluation.

Of the 36 respondents who attempted the SUS survey, 30 were validated after screening — 5 were excluded for insufficient app usage time and 1 for straight-line response bias. This screening ensures data quality.

Why is it called "quantitative descriptive developmental" and not mixed-methods? ▼

Mixed-methods research integrates both quantitative and qualitative data collection — typically combining surveys with interviews, focus groups, or observations. This study collected no qualitative data. No interviews were conducted. All instruments were structured questionnaires yielding numerical data analyzed through frequency, percentage, and SUS scoring.

The design is correctly described as quantitative descriptive developmental: quantitative because all data are numerical, descriptive because it describes financial practices and usability through survey data, and developmental because it involves the design, development, and evaluation of a working system.

What is the Financial Health Score and is it validated? ▼

The Financial Health Score (FHS) is a composite indicator ranging from 0 to 100 computed from four equally weighted components: Savings Rate, Overspend Control, Budget Adherence, and Logging Consistency — each contributing 25 points.

It is formally classified as a Prototype Observed Financial Health Indicator — it is not claimed to be a validated psychometric instrument. Its design adapts two published frameworks: the Commonwealth Bank of Australia and Melbourne Institute's observed scale (Comerton-Forde et al., 2018), which uses transaction records rather than surveys, and the Financial Health Network's FinHealth Score® (Financial Health Network, 2021). The equal-weighting approach mirrors CBA's own published methodology.

We explicitly state its prototype status throughout the manuscript to avoid overstating its validity.

Why Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite specifically? ▼

Eight candidate LLM APIs were evaluated using standardized Filipino-English prompts weighted across five criteria: Filipino-English accuracy (25%), speed/latency (20%), tool use and JSON reliability (20%), free tier availability (15%), and context window (10%) and financial reasoning (10%).

Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite was selected because it offered the highest free-tier request quota (~500 requests/day), a 1-million token context window, native function calling for all 34 agentic action types, and the best Filipino-English (Taglish) handling in team trials. Paid-only models like GPT-4o and Claude were cost-prohibitive for academic zero-budget deployment.

How does SmartSpend handle data privacy? ▼

SmartSpend implements on-device redaction before any data is sent to a cloud AI provider. Philippine mobile numbers, GCash/bank reference numbers, and account patterns are automatically masked with placeholder tokens (e.g., [REDACTED_MOBILE]) before transmission.

Users who prefer full privacy can enable Local AI Mode, which routes all AI requests to a model on their own home network — no data leaves the device. A one-time privacy disclosure is shown before the user's first AI message. Core financial data remains in the on-device SQLite database; Firebase sync is scoped to the authenticated user's own UID.

This is a risk-reduction measure — not a claim of full legal compliance with RA 10173, which requires organizational measures beyond a capstone prototype's scope.

What are the main limitations of SmartSpend? ▼

SmartSpend is limited to Android devices only. Direct banking integration was excluded due to the absence of open-banking APIs in the Philippine setting and data privacy concerns. The application does not provide professional financial or investment advice. OCR accuracy depends on receipt image quality, and voice recognition is affected by background noise. All AI features require an active internet connection unless Local AI Mode is configured. No interviews were conducted — all data are quantitative survey-based.

What is the difference between SmartSpend and GCash Pera Coach / existing apps? ▼

GCash Pera Coach answers financial literacy questions on demand but does not track expenses or compute a health score. Tarsi supports offline OCR receipt tracking but has no conversational AI. YNAB requires manual entry and a paid subscription. Monarch Money requires bank integration unavailable in the Philippines.

SmartSpend's unique combination is: Taglish conversational AI + Financial Health Score + multi-modal input (voice, OCR, barcode, batch screenshots) + offline-first operation — in a single free Android application. No other evaluated app offered all four together and was put through the same hands-on test protocol.

Why was purposive sampling used for the content validator but random for respondents? ▼

The 30 respondents were selected through random sampling — any parent aged 35–55 or young professional aged 21–35 who met the inclusion criteria was eligible, and selection was not based on any specific expertise.

The content validator (Avigail Candevario, PFMe) was selected through purposive sampling because content validation requires a subject matter expert in financial management. The validator's specific professional qualifications — a BS Commerce graduate and active business owner with financial management experience — were the basis for selection, making purposive sampling the appropriate approach.

What does "partially achieved" mean for Objective 3? ▼

The third objective was to determine the usability of SmartSpend using the SUS. The objective was partially achieved because: the evaluation was successfully conducted with 30 valid respondents, the SUS scores were computed and analyzed, and the results are fully documented. However, the mean score of 61.83 fell below the pre-established acceptance threshold of 80 and below the 68-point average benchmark, indicating that the application did not reach the target usability level for this prototype stage.

The partial achievement acknowledges that the process was completed correctly but the outcome criterion was not met — which is a valid and honest research finding, not a failure of methodology.
