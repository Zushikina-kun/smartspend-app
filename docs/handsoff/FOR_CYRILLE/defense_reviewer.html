Financial Health Score (FHS) — 4 Components × 25 Points Each

1. Savings Rate
Score based on 20% savings target (50/30/20 rule, Warren & Tyagi 2005)
2. Overspend Control
Tracks habitual vs. one-off overspend days (half-weight for one-offs)
3. Budget Adherence
On-budget categories ÷ total budgeted categories for the period
4. Logging Consistency
Logged days ÷ active days this period (promotes daily habit)
FHS is formally classified as a Prototype Observed Financial Health Indicator — adapted from CBA-MI (Comerton-Forde et al., 2018) and FinHealth Network (2021). It is NOT a validated psychometric instrument. This is stated explicitly throughout the manuscript.

💡 9 Recommendations

1
Payday-cycle-aware budgeting

Implement budget resets aligned with the Philippine semi-monthly (15th and 30th) salary cycle to better match the financial rhythm of target users.

2
Backend API proxy and per-user keys

Move LLM API key management to a server-side proxy (e.g., Firebase Cloud Function) to remove device-side key exposure. Free-tier quotas are currently shared by all users.

3
Google Play release

After backend proxy and privacy policy are in place, submit SmartSpend to the Google Play Store. Currently distributed as a downloadable APK.

4
On-device AI tier

Add an opt-in on-device model (e.g., Gemma 4 E2B via Google's LiteRT-LM, ~2.6 GB download) for users who want fully offline AI without any network dependency.

5
SQLite encryption

Implement SQLCipher-based encryption for the local database in a future schema migration. The current prototype database is unencrypted.

6
Household and remittance features

Add shared couple/family wallets and OFW remittance tracking as a distinct income category to serve a broader range of Filipino household structures.

7
Longitudinal evaluation

Conduct a follow-up study measuring SmartSpend's effect on actual financial behavior over 3–6 months with a larger sample. Current SUS measured usability at a single point in time.

8
UI/UX refinement From SUS

Address the main usability concerns from SUS: system consistency (Q6) and complexity (Q2, Q4) through interface redesign in future iterations.

9
Formal parsing evaluation

Develop a benchmark of 50+ standardized receipt images across multiple platforms and formally measure OCR and AI parsing precision and recall.

SUS / ResultsWhy did SmartSpend score only 61.83 — is that a failure?▼

The score of 61.83 falls within the Marginal range (Bangor et al., 2008) and corresponds to an adjective rating of "OK" (Bangor et al., 2009). While it is below our pre-established target of 80 and below the 68-point average benchmark (Sauro & Lewis, 2016), it does not constitute a failure.

SmartSpend is a prototype, and prototype-stage systems commonly yield Marginal SUS scores. The evaluation successfully identified specific areas for improvement — particularly system consistency (Q6) and complexity (Q2 and Q4) — which directly informed Recommendations 8 and 9.

The third objective was partially achieved: usability was evaluated with 30 valid respondents, scores were computed and analyzed, and the gap from target is documented and addressed in our recommendations.

MethodologyWhy only 30 respondents? Is that statistically sufficient?▼

Thirty respondents is aligned with established practice for capstone-level usability and needs-assessment studies (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2011). For SUS specifically, Nielsen (1993) demonstrated that as few as 5 respondents can identify the majority of usability issues, and studies published in Atlantis Press (2026) support 20–30 as sufficient for formative usability evaluation.

Of the 36 respondents who attempted the SUS survey, 30 were validated after screening — 5 were excluded for insufficient app usage time (less than 10 minutes) and 1 for straight-line response bias. This screening process ensures data quality and validity.

MethodologyWhy is it quantitative descriptive developmental and not mixed-methods?▼

Mixed-methods research integrates both quantitative and qualitative data collection — typically combining surveys with interviews, focus groups, or observations. This study collected no qualitative data. No interviews were conducted. All instruments were structured questionnaires yielding numerical data analyzed through frequency, percentage, and SUS scoring.

The design is correctly described as quantitative descriptive developmental: quantitative because all data are numerical, descriptive because it describes financial practices and usability through survey data, and developmental because it involves the design, development, and evaluation of a working system (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2011).

FHSWhat is the Financial Health Score and is it a validated instrument?▼

The Financial Health Score is a composite indicator ranging from 0 to 100 computed from four equally weighted components: Savings Rate, Overspend Control, Budget Adherence, and Logging Consistency — each contributing 25 points.

It is formally classified as a Prototype Observed Financial Health Indicator — it is NOT a validated psychometric instrument. Its design adapts two published frameworks: the Commonwealth Bank of Australia and Melbourne Institute's observed scale (Comerton-Forde et al., 2018), which uses transaction records rather than surveys, and the Financial Health Network's FinHealth Score® (2021). Equal weighting mirrors CBA's own published methodology.

We explicitly state its prototype status throughout the manuscript to avoid overstating its validity.

AI / TechWhy Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite specifically? Why not ChatGPT or Claude?▼

Eight candidate LLM APIs were evaluated using standardized Filipino-English prompts across five criteria: Filipino-English accuracy (25%), speed/latency (20%), tool use and JSON reliability (20%), free tier availability (15%), context window (10%), and financial reasoning (10%).

Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite was selected because it offered the highest free-tier request quota (~500 requests/day), a 1-million token context window, native function calling for all 34 agentic action types, and the best Filipino-English (Taglish) handling in team trials.

GPT-4o and Claude were paid-only with no meaningful free tier — cost-prohibitive for academic zero-budget deployment. The 7 fallback providers in the chain ensure availability if Gemini is rate-limited.

PrivacyHow does SmartSpend handle data privacy and RA 10173?▼

SmartSpend implements on-device redaction before any data is sent to a cloud AI provider. Philippine mobile numbers, GCash/bank reference numbers, and account patterns are automatically masked with placeholder tokens (e.g., [REDACTED_MOBILE]) before transmission.

Users who prefer full privacy can enable Local AI Mode, which routes all AI requests to a model on their own home network — no financial data leaves the device. A one-time privacy disclosure is shown before the user's first AI message.

This is a risk-reduction measure — not a claim of full legal compliance with RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012), which requires organizational and legal measures beyond what a capstone prototype can establish.

ScopeWhat are the main limitations?▼

SmartSpend is limited to Android devices only. Direct banking integration was excluded due to the absence of open-banking APIs in the Philippine setting and data privacy concerns. The application does not provide professional financial or investment advice.

OCR accuracy depends on receipt image quality. Voice recognition is affected by background noise. All AI features require an active internet connection unless Local AI Mode is configured. The study used structured surveys only — no interviews were conducted.

ComparisonWhat makes SmartSpend different from GCash Pera Coach and other apps?▼

GCash Pera Coach answers financial literacy questions on demand but does not track expenses or compute a health score. Tarsi supports offline OCR receipt tracking but has no conversational AI. YNAB requires manual entry and a paid subscription. Monarch Money requires bank integration unavailable in the Philippines.

SmartSpend's unique combination is: Taglish conversational AI + Financial Health Score + multi-modal input (voice, OCR, barcode, batch screenshots) + offline-first operation — in a single free Android application evaluated with real users. No other compared app offered all four together and was put through the same hands-on test protocol.

SamplingWhy different sampling for respondents vs. content validator?▼

The 30 respondents were selected through random sampling — any parent aged 35–55 or young professional aged 21–35 who met the inclusion criteria was eligible, with no specific expertise required.

The content validator (Avigail Candevario, PFMe) was selected through purposive sampling because content validation requires a subject matter expert in financial management. Her specific credentials — BS Commerce graduate, active business owner with financial management experience — were the selection basis.

ResultsWhat does "partially achieved" mean for Objective 3?▼

The third objective was to determine the usability of SmartSpend. It was partially achieved because: (1) the evaluation was successfully conducted with 30 valid respondents; (2) SUS scores were computed using the standard Brooke (1996) formula; (3) results are fully documented and analyzed. However, the mean score of 61.83 fell below the pre-established acceptance threshold of 80 and below the 68-point average benchmark.

Partial achievement acknowledges that the process was completed correctly but the outcome criterion was not met — which is a valid research finding, not a failure of methodology.

AI / TechWhat about the token exhaustion issue with long messages?▼

When users send long paragraphs to Peso, the context payload (~7,600+ prompt tokens from 278 expenses, payment plans, goals, and chat history) leaves only 39–95 completion tokens — not enough to execute multiple agentic actions in one response.

This is a known constraint of the current context injection approach. Workarounds: send one item per message, use quick-log chips for repeat expenses, keep messages short and structured. This is documented as a limitation in Recommendation 2 (backend proxy) and Recommendation 7 (longitudinal evaluation with larger-scale testing).

A proper fix would involve context summarization or a sliding window for older expenses — this is noted as a post-capstone engineering improvement.
